INTRODUCTION
Genetic and acquired disorders affecting lung function contribute to
the global burden of lung disease. Mutations in genes critical for
normal functions of pulmonary epithelial, endothelial, and mesen-
chymal cells cause a number of debilitating conditions, including
cystic ﬁbrosis, surfactant protein disorders, ciliary dyskinesia, and
pulmonary phospholipid clearance disorders. An intact form of a
number of genes, including SFTPC,
1 SFTPB,2 NKX2-1,3 ABCA3,4
CFTR,5 TBX4,6 and FOXF1,7 is essential for lung formation or func-
tion.8 Mutations or dysregulation of these genes cause life-threatening
diffuse pulmonary disorders in infants and children.
Recent advances in nanoparticle development have shown signi ﬁcant
promise. Technologies such as organ-targeting lipids (e.g., selective
organ targeting [SORT]),
9– 12 preformed vesicles (PFV)-lipid nano-
particles (LNPs),13 and polymer-LNPs14– 16 present substantial poten-
tial for gene therapy. SORT LNPs, for instance, exhibit remarkable
lung-targeting ability, enhanced by increased 1,2-dioleoyl-3-trime-
thylammonium propane (DOTAP) to improve lung targeting via
intravenous (IV) administration and include ionizable amino lipids
and helper lipids to enhance transfection ef ﬁciency. SORT LNPs
have demonstrated both lung endothelial and epithelial transfection
ability.
9– 11 Similarly, SM-102 was shown to improve lung transfection
efﬁciency.12 Poly(b-amino ester)s (PBAEs) have also been tested for
delivering DNA and mRNA into lungs by intratracheal (IT) or IV in-
jection.
17,18 The combination of PBAE and LNPs comprising 1,2-di-
oleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphoethanolamine (DOPE)/cholesterol/C14-
polyethylene glycol (PEG) was tested for delivering mRNA into lung
endothelial cells by IV injection.
15 These LNPs and PBAE exhibit low
toxicity, biocompatibility, and biodegradability compared with poly-
ethylenimine-based and poly-
L-lysine-based nanoparticles. 12,19,20
However, the combination of PBAE-PEG (or PBAE) and SORT lipids
(4A3-SC8/DOPE/cholesterol/DOTAP; hereafter, LNP) to create
PBAE-PEG/LNP or PBAE/LNP has not been extensively explored.
In the present study, we synthesized two distinct types of PBAEs,
PBAE-PEG and PBAE, and combined them with LNPs to produce
two unique nanoparticle formulations that we tested in vitro and
in vivo. IV injection of both PBAE-PEG/LNP and PBAE/LNP selec-
tively delivered mRNA into pulmonary endothelial cells. IT adminis-
tration of these nanoparticles demonstrated that PBAE-PEG/LNP

RESULTS
Synthesis of PBAE and PBAE-PEG
Synthesis of PBAE- or PBAE-PEG-based LNPs was accomplished
using methods and techniques that were previously reported by Mas-
torakos et al.
17 Brieﬂy, PBAE was synthesized through step-growth
polymerization via Michael addition to facilitate the reaction be-
tween 1,4-butanediol diacrylate and 4-amino-1-butanol. PBAE was
then capped by applying hydrophilic 1,3-diaminopropane capping
agent. As the PBAE backbone is relatively hydrophobic, this resulted
in the creation of amphipathic polymers ( Figure 1A). Finally, PBAE-
PEG was made by reacting the capped terminal primary amine
groups of the PBAE polymer chain with methoxy-PEG-succinimidyl
succinates ( Figure 1 A). Reaction completion was veri ﬁed by
1H
NMR ( Figures 1 B and 1C). For capped PBAE, the chemical shift
d = 1.59 ppm corresponds to the resonance of the methylene protons
beta to the nitrogen in the PBAE backbone, while the chemical shifts
between 1.19 and 1.25 ppm suggest the presence of protons of 1,3-
diaminopropane ( Figure 1 B). For PBAE-PEG, the chemical shift
d = 3.59 ppm corresponds to the protons of methylene and methyl
groups of PEG, indicating the successful formation of PBAE-PEG.
The molecular weight of capped PBAE was determined by end-
group analysis using
1H NMR spectra ( Figure 1 C) and calculated
to be /C244,400 g/mol ( Table S1 ), which is close to the value reported
by Mastorakos et al. 17

Next, we sought to determine which mass ratio between PBAE-PEG/
LNP (or PBAE/LNP) and mRNA provides the best transfection ef ﬁ-
ciency using luciferase mRNA in HEK293T cells in vitro . Ratios of
30:1 and 60:1 (PBAE-PEG/LNP:mRNA or PBAE/LNP:mRNA) pro-
vided the highest transfection efﬁciency as measured by luciferase activ-
ity compared to those of 10:1 or 90:1 (Figure 2D). The size and surface
charge of PBAE-PEG/LNP or PBAE/LNP with different amounts of
luciferase mRNA (unmodiﬁed) was determined by Zetasizer. The size
of PBAE-PEG/LNP itself was 184.3 nm. When assembled with the
mRNA at a mass ratio of 10:1 (PBAE-PEG/LNP:mRNA), the size signif-
icantly increased to 245.6 nm. When assembled with mRNA at a mass
ratio of 30:1 or higher (PBAE-PEG/LNP:mRNA), the size signiﬁcantly
decreased to below 144.3 nm ( Figure 2E). Likewise, the size of PBAE/
LNP alone was 208 nm. When assembled with mRNA at a mass ratio
of 10:1 (PBAE/LNP:mRNA), the size signi ﬁcantly increased to
260.3 nm. When assembled with mRNA at a mass ratio of 30:1 or higher
(PBAE/LNP:mRNA), the size signiﬁcantly decreased to below 176 nm
(Figure 2E). The zeta potential of PBAE-PEG/LNP or PBAE/LNP itself
was 24.6 or 41.8 mV, respectively. When assembled with mRNA at a
mass ratio of 30:1, the zeta potential of PBAE-PEG/LNP/mRNA or
PBAE/LNP/mRNA was 0.77 and 20.2 mV, respectively. When assem-
bled with mRNA at a mass ratio of 60:1 and 90:1, the zeta potential of
PBAE-PEG/LNP/mRNA or PBAE/LNP/mRNA was higher at 10.3
and 27.4 mV, respectively ( Figure 2F). These in vitro results suggest
that PBAE-PEG/LNP or PBAE/LNP with mRNA at the mass ratio of
30:1 or 60:1 is applicable for in vivo studies. Due to cost-effectiveness,
we chose PBAE-PEG/LNP or PBAE/LNP with mRNA at a mass ratio
of 30:1 for the further in vitro and in vivo studies below.
We assessed the morphological characteristics of PBAE-PEG/LNP or
PBAE/LNP with mRNA. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)
indicated that PBAE-PEG/LNP and PBAE/LNP demonstrate a meso-
porous structure with sizes of 211 and 204 nm, respectively (Figure 2G).
To predict how PBAE-PEG/LNP releases mRNA after entering cells,
we measured the size of PBAE-PEG/LNP in PBS at pH 7 and 100 or
10 mM citric acid buffer at pH 6 and pH 5. At neutral pH 7, PBAE-
PEG/LNP exhibited an average size of 145.2 nm (polydispersity index
[PDI] = 0.137). As the pH decreased, the size increased progressively
from 293.0 nm (PDI = 0.266) at pH 6 to 667.9 nm (PDI = 0.562) at
pH 5 ( Figure 2 H), indicating that PBAE-PEG/LNP is responsive to
pH change. Consistent with these size distribution results, mRNA
was released from PBAE-PEG/LNP at a lower pH (pH 4 – 6) but not
at pH 7 (Figure 2I). These results suggest that PBAE-PEG/LNP releases
mRNA in the endocytosis process at low pH.
In vitro transfection validation of PBAE-PEG/LNP and PBAE/LNP
with 5-methoxyuridine modiﬁed mRNA
To be applicable for in vivo studies, since 5-methoxyuridine modiﬁed
(5moU) mRNA is less immunogenic than unmodi ﬁed mRNA, 21 we
tested PBAE-PEG/LNP encapsulating 5moU luciferase mRNA along
with unmodiﬁed mRNA in HEK293T cells in vitro. PBAE-PEG/LNP
with 5moU luciferase mRNA signi ﬁcantly induced luciferase activity
similar to that achieved with unmodi ﬁed mRNA ( Figure 3 A), indi-
cating that PBAE-PEG/LNP can deliver 5moU mRNA as well as un-
modiﬁed mRNA. The transfection ef ﬁciency of PBAE-PEG/LNP or
PBAE/LNP with 5moU mRNA was also con ﬁrmed by ﬂuorescence
analysis using 5moU mCherry mRNA in HEK293T cells in vitro ,
which further indicated that PBAE-PEG/LNP is superior to PBAE/
LNP in transfecting HEK293T cells ( Figures 3B and 3C).

FACS data indicated that IT injection of PBAE-PEG/LNP or PBAE/
LNP with 5moU mRNA delivers mRNA into EpCAM (CD326)+ lung
epithelial cells ( Figure 5). We used confocal immuno ﬂuorescence to
determine which lung epithelial cell types are transfected with
5moU Cre mRNA in Ai14 tdTomato mice by IT injection. While
PBAE-PEG/LNP did not induce tdTomato expression in ERG +
lung endothelial cells ( Figures 7A and S8), PBAE-PEG/LNP induced
tdTomato expression in both LAMP3/NKX2-1 + lung ATII epithelial
cells ( Figures 7 B, S9, and S10) and SCGB1A1 + lung airway club
epithelial cells ( Figures 7 C, S10, and S11). PBAE-PEG/LNP did not
induce tdTomato expression in TUBA1A + lung airway ciliated
epithelial cells ( Figures 7 C and S11) or MEOX2 + lung ﬁbroblasts
(Figures 7D and S12).
While PBAE/LNP did not induce tdTomato expression in ERG + lung
endothelial cells either ( Figures 7 A and S8), PBAE/LNP induced
tdTomato expression signi ﬁcantly more in SCGB1A1 + lung airway
club epithelial cells ( Figures 7 C, S10, and S11) than in LAMP3/
NKX2-1+ lung ATII epithelial cells ( Figures 7 B, S9, and S10).
PBAE/LNP did not induce tdTomato expression in TUBA1A + lung
airway ciliated epithelial cells ( Figures 7 C and S11) or MEOX2 +
lung ﬁbroblasts ( Figures 7 D and S12). These data indicate that
PBAE-PEG/LNP administered by in vivo IT injection is superior
for the targeting of both lung ATII epithelial cells and airway club
epithelial cells, while PBAE/LNP is superior for speci ﬁcally targeting
lung airway club epithelial cells over lung ATII epithelial cells.
DISCUSSION
Despite advances in nanoparticle technology, pulmonary cell-speci ﬁc
delivery systems remain challenging and have not been clinically
applied yet. In our study, we developed two nanoparticles, PBAE-
PEG/LNP and PBAE/LNP, to deliver mRNA into lung endothelial
cells via IV injection and into lung epithelial cells, including lung
ATII cells and airway club cells, via IT injection without causing liver
toxicity and lung in ﬂammation. Our results demonstrated that
PEGylation affects targeting ability. Speci ﬁcally, PBAE-PEG/LNP
delivered mRNA into lung epithelial cells, including ATII and airway
club cells, while PBAE/LNP targeted airway club cells better than
ATII cells via IT injection.
In this study, PEGylation of PBAE facilitated delivery of mRNA into
lung ATII epithelial cells beyond airway club epithelial cells. We hy-
pothesize that PEGylation helps to (1) stabilize LNPs, (2) reduce their
immunogenicity, and (3) prevent phagocytosis by alveolar macro-
phages, which results in wider targeting of lung epithelial cells in
the alveolar regions. PBAE-PEG/LNP can be considered a potential
candidate for delivering therapeutic cargo into ATII epithelial cells
for genetic disorders, for example, ABCA3 de ﬁciency. However,
PBAE/LNP also presents a promising alternative for speci ﬁc pulmo-
nary conditions where targeted gene delivery into airway club epithe-
lial cells, which serve as progenitors of goblet and ciliated epithelial
cells, is needed (e.g., cystic ﬁbrosis).
We have designed a formulation of LNPs for mRNA delivery
based on previously reported LNP formulations.
10,15,17 However,
as reported previously, Dlin-MC3-DMA and SM-102 can be
used instead of 4A3-SC8 for ionizable lipids.
12,23 Different combi-
nations of helper lipids, including DOPE, distearoylphosphatidyl-
choline, dimyristoyl glycerol-PEG, and C14-PEG, may improve
the transfection ef ﬁciency.
10,15 For further optimization, probably
the most ef ﬁcient approach would be to combine machine learning
with high-throughput lipid synthesis, 24 which might provide the
best LNP formulation for IT and/or IV administration of mRNA
in the future.
For the COVID-19 vaccine, N1-methylpseudouridine-modi ﬁed
mRNA was used to reduce mRNA immunogenicity and increase
the half-life of synthetic mRNA.
25,26 In our study, we used 5moU
mRNA commercially available from TriLink, which provided suc-
cessful transfection ef ﬁciency with PBAE-PEG/LNP and PBAE/
LNP in vivo . However, N1-methylpseudouridine-modi ﬁed mRNA
might provide better transfection ef ﬁciency considering that protein
expression from N1-methylpseudouridine-modiﬁed mRNA is higher
than that from 5moU mRNA and the immunogenicity from N1-
methylpseudouridine-modiﬁed mRNA is lower than that from
5moU mRNA according to the products at the TriLink Biotechnol-
ogies site ( https://www.trilinkbiotech.com/browse/stocked-mrnas).
We will test N1-methylpseudouridine-modi ﬁed mRNA with PBAE-
PEG/LNP and PBAE/LNP in a future study.
In summary, our work proposes that PBAE-PEG/LNP and PBAE/
LNP have different targeting abilities after IT or IV injection. Our
study demonstrated the possibility of selective gene transfer for future
therapeutic mRNA or genome editing via Cas9 mRNA and guide
RNA(s) into pulmonary endothelial and epithelial cells. Our study
provides a framework for designing temporary or permanent strate-
gies for pulmonary gene therapy for disorders caused by mutations in
ABCA3, CFTR, SFTPB, and SFTPC, and other monogenic lung dis-
eases. Further studies are needed to assess the duration of expression
and whether levels of protein expression will be suf ﬁcient to be an
effective treatment to genetic disorders.

Synthesis of PBAE and PEGylated PBAE
Synthesis of the PBAE polymer backbone was conducted via a two-
step Michael addition reaction, where 1,4-butanediol diacrylate and
4-amino-1-butanol were combined in a 1.2:1 molar ratio and reacted
at 90
/C14C for 24 h to produce uncapped PBAE polymers. These poly-
mers were subsequently precipitated three times in cold ether to elim-
inate any unreacted materials and then dried under vacuum. For the
capping of the terminal acrylate groups, the PBAE polymers were sol-
ubilized in tetrahydrofuran (THF) at a concentration of 100 mg/mL,
and 30 molar equiv of 1,3-diaminopropane were introduced. The re-
action was carried out with stirring at room temperature for 4 to 5 h.
Finally, the end-capped PBAE polymers were isolated by precipita-
tion in cold ether to remove any unreacted reagents and then dried
under vacuum. Methoxy-PEG-succinimidyl succinates were then
conjugated to the two terminal primary amine groups on both ends
of the 1,3-diaminopropane-capped PBAE polymer chain. This pro-
cess involved transferring the capped PBAE polymers along with
2.05 molar equiv of 5 kDa methoxy-PEG-succinimidyl succinate
into a glass vial, which was then evacuated and ﬁlled with nitrogen.
The reactants were dissolved in THF, and the mixture was stirred
at room temperature overnight. The resultant PBAE-PEG polymer
was then precipitated and washed three times with cold ether,
followed by drying under vacuum. PBAE polymers were stored
at /C020
/C14C.
Creation of PBAE-PEG/LNP and PBAE/LNP
LNP without PBAE-PEG or PBAE was prepared by dissolving
4A3-SC8, DOPE, cholesterol, and DOTAP at a molar ratio of
20:20:35:60 in ethanol. PBAE-PEG/LNP and PBAE/LNP were pre-
pared by dissolving lipids (4A3-SC8, DOPE, cholesterol, and
DOTAP) along with PBAE-PEG or PBAE in ethanol at the molar ra-
tio of 20:20:35:60:2.5. Concurrently, mRNA was dissolved in a 10 mM
citrate buffer at pH 4. The lipids in ethanol and the RNA in the citrate
buffer were then rapidly mixed using a vortex as described previ-
ously
10 at an ethanol-aqueous volume ratio of 1:3. This mixture
was incubated at room temperature for 10 min and then dialyzed
in PBS at pH 7 overnight, which achieved a ﬁnal weight ratio of
30:1 (total lipids:mRNA). The PBAE-PEG/LNP formulation com-
prises an LNP composition that includes 4A3-SC8, DOPE, choles-
terol, PBAE-PEG, and DOTAP in a molar ratio of 20:20:35:2.5:60,
respectively. The PBAE/LNP formulation consists of 4A3-SC8,
DOPE, cholesterol, PBAE, and DOTAP, also in a molar ratio of
20:20:35:2.5:60.
