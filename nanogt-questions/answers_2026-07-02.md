# nanogt Answer Sheet — 2026-07-02

**1. What is a "nano-rare" disease, and what is the core bet nanogt makes?**
A nano-rare disease is one so rare that it will essentially never justify its own dedicated pivotal trial or bespoke platform development — the patient population is too small to attract standalone investment or generate independent evidence. nanogt's core bet is that regulatory and translational risk can be *borrowed*: if you can identify an existing, already-validated GT program that shares the deciding biological and delivery features (right vector, right tissue, right protein class, compatible mechanism), that program acts as a de-risking precedent — a template for construct design, delivery route, tox package and regulatory argument. The tool operationalises "find the closest existing precedent" as a ranked, dimension-by-dimension comparison rather than an expert hunch.

**2. Modalities covered vs excluded, and why naive scoring is unfair to excluded diseases.**
The catalog covers only AAV-based in vivo gene addition and ex vivo lentiviral / integrating-HSC gene addition (Strimvelis is gammaretroviral but grouped in the integrating-HSC bucket). It explicitly excludes: dual-AAV strategies, LNP/mRNA gene therapy, genome editing (CRISPR/base/prime), antisense oligonucleotides, RNAi, and cell+gene combination approaches. A disease whose real path is, say, base editing (a dominant-negative disorder) or an ASO (a splice defect) will score *low* here simply because no in-scope precedent resembles it — but that low score reflects the *catalog's* boundaries, not the disease's true tractability. Reading the number naively would wrongly label a genuinely treatable disease as untreatable.

**3. Why confidence and review_flags are deliberately separate.**
Confidence (high/medium/low) is a purely quantitative statement about *how strong the precedent match is within the catalog* — it is set on match_score alone. review_flags are qualitative translational caveats that ranking cannot capture: MTS-import validation needed, lysosomal-membrane protein (cross-correction impossible), unresolved gene function, multi-subunit complex, etc. Keeping them separate means a program can legitimately be the best available catalog analogue (high confidence) while still carrying a flag that says "this analogy hides a biological step that must be independently proven." Collapsing the two would either hide real caveats or unfairly crush the ranking of an otherwise-reasonable precedent.

**4. Raw-max values and why they normalise separately.**
MATCH_RAW_MAX = 17.0, built from the weighted maxima of the eight program-comparative dimensions: packaging 2.0×1.5=3, tropism 2.0×1.5=3, modality 2.0×1.5=3, protein_class 2.0×1.0=2, pathway 2.0×1.0=2, inheritance 1.0×1.0=1, approval 1.0×1.0=1, immunogenicity 2.0×1.0=2 → 3+3+3+2+2+1+1+2 = 17. TRACTABILITY_RAW_MAX = 7.0, from the six disease-intrinsic dimensions: therapeutic_window 2.0 + cross_correction 1.0 + immune_privilege 1.0 + promoter 1.0 + roa 1.0 + organelle 1.0 = 7. They must be normalised (and reported) separately because they answer different questions — "how good is this precedent?" vs "how amenable is this disease?" — and the tractability dimensions are identical across every candidate, so summing them back in would reintroduce exactly the v3 flaw the v4 split was designed to remove.

**5. Justification for the three 1.5× go/no-go gates.**
packaging_fit: a gene whose coding sequence exceeds the capsid simply cannot be delivered by that platform — predicts outright delivery failure (or forces a different modality). tropism_match: if the vector does not reach the target cell in vivo, no amount of good payload matters — predicts zero transduction of the relevant tissue. modality_compatibility: gain-of-function, dominant-negative and toxic-gain-of-RNA mechanisms make gene *addition* the wrong tool entirely — predicts that adding wild-type copies fails to correct (or worsens) the phenotype. All three are failure modes that cannot be engineered around without changing the fundamental strategy, which is why they are weighted above the "workaround exists" dimensions.

**6. Immunogenicity bands and the four caveats.**
Bands: seroprevalence <10% → 2.0 (low; most patients eligible); <20% → 1.5 (moderate; pre-screening/exclusion); <40% → 1.0 (high; substantial exclusion, immunodepletion may be needed); ≥40% → 0.5 (very high; majority may be ineligible). The four caveats making these relative rather than absolute: (a) geographic/ancestry variation (AAV1/AAV6 seroprevalence higher in sub-Saharan Africa and SE Asia); (b) capsid engineering — synthetic/evolved capsids have different NAb profiles than wild-type serotypes; (c) paediatric dosing — neonates/infants have lower seroprevalence than the Western-adult cohorts these values come from; (d) immunodepletion protocols (plasmapheresis, IVIg, rapamycin) can expand eligibility. So the intended reading is the ordering LV < AAV5 < AAVrh10 < AAV2, not a hard clinical cutoff.

**7. Hemophilia B (~9.9) vs Fabry (~8.7–8.9).**
Hemophilia B (F9, 1383 bp, liver, secreted) is close to the ideal case for this catalog: small gene (comfortable packaging), liver tropism directly matched by Hemgenix/Roctavian (perfect tropism), a genuinely *secreted* protein (max protein-class and cross-correction), same coagulation pathway, and an approved liver precedent. Almost every match dimension maxes out, and liver is a favourable tractability tissue. Fabry (GLA, secreted lysosomal enzyme) is also strong but is multi-system (liver/kidney/heart/CNS): its tropism and the tissue-averaged tractability dimensions (immune privilege, promoter, roa) are pulled down by harder tissues like kidney and CNS, and the best precedent is an HSC/LV or hybrid program rather than a same-indication approved product. The separating dimensions are chiefly tropism_match and approval_weight (match bucket) plus the tissue-averaged tractability dimensions (tractability bucket).

**8. Why Crigler-Najjar (~7.2) trails Hemophilia B despite both being liver LOF.**
The gene fit is comparable, but the precedent quality and protein biology differ. Crigler-Najjar's UGT1A1 is an ER-membrane / intracellular hepatic enzyme, not a *secreted* plasma protein like Factor IX — so it scores lower on protein_class and, critically, on cross_correction (an intracellular/membrane hepatic enzyme cannot rescue neighbouring untransduced hepatocytes the way secreted FIX benefits the whole bloodstream). The top precedent (Hemgenix-class) is a liver program for a *different* protein class, so tropism/pathway match is decent but protein-class and cross-correction are only partial, and there is no same-indication approved product to max approval_weight. Those protein-class and cross-correction deficits, not delivery, drive the ~2.7-point gap.

**9. Why an HSC/LV precedent (Libmeldy) can top a CNS disease (Sanfilippo A).**
SGSH is a *soluble, secretable* lysosomal enzyme, so it is amenable to cross-correction via the M6P pathway — and that is precisely the mechanism Libmeldy exploits: HSC-derived myeloid cells engraft, migrate into the CNS, secrete the enzyme, and neighbouring neurons take it up. So the match is driven by protein_class (both soluble lysosomal enzymes → 2.0), pathway (lysosomal/leukodystrophy group), cross-correction potential, and an *approved* status, which together outweigh the fact that it isn't a direct AAV9-CNS program. You'd still expect review flags around CNS delivery/route feasibility and the general caveat that a top precedent is a platform comparison, not a validated ready-made construct.

**10. `_aggregate_tissue_mean`: which three dimensions, the v4 change, and `--primary-tissue`.**
It backs immune_privilege, promoter_availability and roa_feasibility. In v4 these switched from returning the single *most favourable* tissue's score to the *mean across all annotated tissues*, so a multi-system disease can no longer be over-scored by cherry-picking its easiest tissue while its real therapeutic target is ignored. `--primary-tissue` lets you declare one target tissue; since the mean of a single value is just that value, it collapses the aggregate to that one tissue's score — e.g. scoring Fabry as a liver-directed program (liver's high scores) rather than diluting/averaging across kidney, heart and CNS.

**11. The two 0.0 triggers in `score_organelle_targeting`.**
Trigger 1: gene symbol starts with "MT-" (the HGNC convention for mitochondrial-DNA-encoded genes, e.g. MT-ND4), length > 3. Trigger 2: the disease's inheritance field contains "mitochondrial" (maternally inherited). The code catches the inheritance case even when the symbol wouldn't flag it because curated/aliased data may not use the strict "MT-" prefix, and maternal inheritance is itself strong evidence the causal gene is mtDNA-encoded. Either way the protein is made by mitochondrial ribosomes with a non-standard code and cannot be produced by nuclear AAV without allotopic expression — a different paradigm (real-world: GS010/Lumevoq for LHON).

**12. `_packaging_gene_for_program` construct swap.**
The swap fires when the program's gene_symbol contains the disease gene symbol *plus* one of the engineered markers "micro", "mini" or "truncated" (e.g. DMD_micro for SRP-9001). For an oversized gene like full-length DMD (~11 kb), the clinically meaningful question is not "does native dystrophin fit an AAV?" (it never will) but "is there precedent for a shortened micro/mini-transgene strategy that fits?" — so scoring packaging against the engineered construct is the correct comparison. Without the swap, every oversized-gene disease would hard-fail packaging against its own engineered precedent, masking the fact that a viable micro-transgene route exists and wrongly excluding legitimate precedents like SRP-9001.

**13. Protein-class 1.0 but cross-correction 0.0 for a lysosomal membrane protein — why the asymmetry.**
Protein class awards *partial* credit (1.0) because the lysosomal *pathway* is genuinely shared with the enzyme precedent — the disease biology sits in the same organelle and the general disease family is related, which is real, if incomplete, similarity. Cross-correction awards *zero* because it scores a specific physical mechanism: a membrane-anchored channel/transporter cannot be exocytosed as soluble cargo and taken up via M6P receptors by neighbouring cells. The shared pathway is partly informative; the secretion mechanism is simply absent. Rewarding the first while zeroing the second encodes exactly that distinction (the v2 fix that stopped MCOLN1/SLC17A5 from inheriting inflated enzyme-precedent scores).

**14. Two bugs in the old hand-written `_PATHWAY_GROUPS`.**
(1) Asymmetry: because it was written by hand, A-related-to-B did not guarantee B-related-to-A, so pathway matches could depend on which side of the comparison a pathway happened to be on. (2) Unsupported cross-links: it contained edges that existed only to make specific results come out, notably retinal_phototransduction ↔ mitochondrial_complex, which was propping up Leber hereditary optic neuropathy (LHON) so it could match retinal programs. v4 builds the adjacency from an undirected edge list (symmetry guaranteed by construction) and drops the unsupported link; LHON is now correctly handled by the organelle-targeting gate instead.

**15. AAV tropism and serotype-as-address.**
Tropism is the set of tissues/cell types a given capsid naturally infects and transduces in vivo — a function of which cell-surface receptors the capsid binds. Because the capsid determines *where* the payload is delivered, choosing a serotype is effectively choosing a delivery address: AAV9 is the broad, CNS-penetrant workhorse (crosses the blood-brain barrier; used in Zolgensma) and also hits muscle/heart/liver; AAV5 is the liver specialist used in the approved hemophilia products (Hemgenix, Roctavian); AAV2 is the retinal/CNS/liver capsid used in Luxturna. Same payload, different capsid → different tissue reached, hence tropism_match is a go/no-go gate.

**16. M6P cross-correction, and where it applies vs cannot.**
Soluble lysosomal enzymes are secreted bearing mannose-6-phosphate tags; neighbouring cells recapture them through M6P receptors and route them to their own lysosomes. This means a *minority* of transduced (or engrafted) cells can supply enzyme to a whole tissue, dramatically lowering the required transduction rate and dose. It applies to soluble lysosomal enzymes such as ARSA (MLD), IDUA (MPS I), GLA (Fabry), SGSH (Sanfilippo A). It physically cannot apply to a lysosomal *membrane* protein such as MCOLN1 (mucolipidosis IV) or the transporter CTNS (cystinosis) / SLC17A5, because a membrane-anchored protein cannot be secreted as soluble cargo — every target cell must be individually transduced.

**17. Withdrawn (0.7) above Phase 2 (0.6): which program and why.**
The withdrawn program is Glybera (alipogene tiparvovec), fully EMA-approved in 2012 and withdrawn in 2017 for *commercial* reasons (price/lack of demand), not safety or efficacy. As a *regulatory precedent* — evidence that regulators have accepted this modality for this indication class — a product that cleared approval is stronger than one still mid-trial, so the code deliberately scores it above an in-progress Phase 2. The code notes that a product withdrawn for *safety or efficacy* reasons would warrant a much lower score and should be encoded separately.

**18. Why tractability_score can't rank precedents yet is worth reporting.**
It cannot rank because it is computed once per disease from disease-intrinsic dimensions and is therefore *identical for every candidate program* — a value that is the same across all options contributes an identical constant to each and cannot change their order. It is still worth reporting because it answers a different, genuinely useful question for the user: independent of *which* precedent you pick, how amenable is this disease to gene therapy at all (window, cross-correction, delivery route, organelle targeting)? A disease with a strong top match but a "challenging" tractability tier is a signal that the precedent similarity may be flattering.

**19. Why HPO-keyword window inference is fragile (beyond PKU).**
Keyword matching over HPO text infers a clinical timing property from the *presence of words*, which fails in several distinct ways. (a) False positives from irreversibility keywords: a term like "intellectual disability" or "progressive" can trip a narrow-window branch even for a disease with a wide treatable window (the PKU failure). (b) Loss of within-disease heterogeneity: a single HPO annotation set cannot distinguish SMA type 1 from type 3, or Gaucher type 1 from type 3, whose windows differ dramatically — the keyword score collapses them. Other failure modes worth crediting: onset words describe *typical* not *earliest treatable* age; absence of a keyword ≠ absence of the feature (annotation gaps); and the heuristic captures no notion of *rate* of progression or *tissue reversibility*. Hence v4 prefers curated natural-history data and only falls back to the heuristic with a review flag.

**20. Defence and honest concessions to the "circular weighted-sum" critique.**
Defence: the value is not in inventing new facts but in making an expert comparison *explicit, consistent, transparent and reproducible*. Every dimension carries a documented rationale and primary citation; the same rules are applied identically to every disease/program pair, so the ranking is auditable and its assumptions are contestable rather than hidden in an expert's head. The v4 match/tractability split, the hard gates, and the review flags show the design actively guards against its own worst failure modes, and the exposed weights let a sensitivity analysis test whether rankings are stable under perturbation — a hand-hunch cannot be stress-tested this way. Concessions the critique correctly identifies: (1) the dimension scores and weights are ultimately hand-assigned expert judgements, so systematic bias in those inputs propagates directly to outputs — the tool cannot discover a consideration it wasn't given a dimension for; and (2) it is a *similarity/precedent* heuristic, not a predictor of clinical success — a high score is a hypothesis to be validated with construct-specific tox, efficacy and regulatory work, not evidence of curability.
