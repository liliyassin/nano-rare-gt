# nanogt Answer Sheet — 2026-06-26

**1. What does nanogt do, and what is the regulatory argument for ranking existing GT programmes?**
nanogt takes an Orphanet disease ID, resolves its causal gene (UniProt) and affected tissues, and scores every gene-therapy programme in a curated catalog as a potential development *precedent* for that disease, returning a ranked list plus a markdown report. The regulatory argument is that for an ultra-rare ("nano-rare") disease there is no dedicated programme and a bespoke trial may be infeasible, so the fastest credible route is to borrow a modality/vector/route that regulators have already accepted for a biologically analogous disease — the closest precedent reduces the unknowns a regulator must evaluate. The tool explicitly does not claim the top precedent is directly reusable without disease-specific validation, toxicology, and regulatory review.

**2. The two v4 scores and the v3 flaw they fixed.**
`match_score` (0–10) measures how good a *precedent* a specific programme is for the disease; `tractability_score` (0–10) measures how amenable the *disease itself* is to gene therapy, independent of which precedent is chosen. In v3 a single composite blended disease-intrinsic dimensions (therapeutic window, cross-correction, immune privilege, etc.) with program-comparative ones. Because the disease-intrinsic dimensions are identical for every candidate programme, they inflated the headline number and the confidence tier without ever changing the ranking — the split removes that contamination.

**3. Which score drives ranking, and why the other must be excluded.**
`match_score` drives both the ranking and the confidence tier. `tractability_score` must be kept out because it is computed once per disease and is identical for every programme; blending it in would add the same constant to every candidate, inflating the headline score and the confidence tier while contributing nothing to discriminating one precedent from another (the exact v3 defect).

**4. The eight match dimensions and the 1.5× three.**
Match dimensions: packaging_fit, tropism_match, modality_compatibility, protein_class, pathway_similarity, inheritance, approval_weight, immunogenicity. The three weighted 1.5× are packaging_fit, tropism_match, and modality_compatibility. Shared justification: each is a clinical go/no-go gate — a gene that can't be packaged, a vector that can't reach the tissue, or a mechanism incompatible with gene addition causes programme failure regardless of every other attribute, and the literature cited (Srivastava 2016; Nathwani 2011; Keeler & Flotte 2019) treats them as primary selection criteria.

**5. The 0.75× tier in v3 and its rationale.**
In v3 the four logistic/administrative dimensions — cross_correction, immune_privilege, promoter_availability, roa_feasibility — were weighted 0.75×. The rationale was that these are solvable engineering/logistics problems (vector engineering, immunosuppression, alternative routes, promoter development) that inform development *complexity* rather than fundamental tractability, so they should contribute less than the go/no-go gates. (Note: in v4 these moved into the separate tractability score, where the exposed `TRACTABILITY_WEIGHTS` are all 1.0×.)

**6. MATCH_RAW_MAX, TRACTABILITY_RAW_MAX, and normalisation.**
`MATCH_RAW_MAX` = 17.0 (3+3+3+2+2+1+1+2) and `TRACTABILITY_RAW_MAX` = 7.0 (2+1+1+1+1+1), each computed as Σ(dimension max × weight). A score is the weighted sum of the dimensions divided by the relevant raw max, times 10, rounded to 2 d.p. — e.g. match_score = weighted_sum × 10/17.

**7. Confidence and tractability thresholds.**
Confidence (on match_score): ≥6.0 high, ≥4.0 medium, <4.0 low; a mechanism or packaging hard-fail forces "fail" (programme excluded). Tractability tier (on tractability_score, advisory only): ≥6.5 amenable, ≥4.0 conditional, <4.0 challenging.

**8. The reordered gates and the Huntington example.**
The mechanism/modality gate is now checked *before* the packaging gate. It fires when gene addition is the wrong modality — gain-of-function, dominant-negative, toxic gain-of-RNA, or overexpression — because adding more copies of the disease gene can't help and may worsen the phenotype. It is the more fundamental exclusion: no vector swap or construct shrink can rescue a wrong modality. For Huntington (HTT), checking mechanism first correctly excludes it on its toxic gain-of-function mechanism, rather than masking that with an incidental packaging failure.

**9. Cassette overhead.**
`_CASSETTE_OVERHEAD_BP` = 1500 bp, representing the regulatory elements packaged around the CDS: two ITRs (~290 bp), a promoter (~250–1000 bp), 5'/3' UTRs + Kozak (~150 bp), and a polyA signal (~150–250 bp). Fill is graded against (CDS + overhead) because that is what actually has to fit the capsid. The absolute hard-fail fires only on the bare CDS because the CDS itself cannot be shortened without re-engineering, whereas overhead can be trimmed (minimal promoter, etc.); a CDS that fits but whose full cassette doesn't gets a low passing score (0.5), not a fail.

**10. Lysosomal enzyme vs lysosomal membrane protein.**
A soluble lysosomal **enzyme** (ARSA, IDUA) matched to a lysosomal precedent scores protein_class 2.0 and cross_correction 1.0 (0.8 if classed as enzyme but not flagged secreted), because it can be secreted and recaptured by neighbouring cells via the mannose-6-phosphate (M6P) receptor pathway. A lysosomal **membrane** protein (MCOLN1, SLC17A5) — a channel/transporter anchored in the membrane — scores protein_class 1.0 and cross_correction 0.0, because it physically cannot be secreted or taken up; every target cell must be individually transduced, so HSC/LV cross-correction precedents (Libmeldy) don't transfer.

**11. Why Hemophilia B scores so high for Hemgenix.**
F9 is small (1383 bp), so packaging_fit maxes (comfortable fit, 2.0, weighted 1.5×). The product is liver-directed and AAV5 is hepatotropic, so tropism_match maxes (vector + precedent target both liver). FIX is a secreted protein matched to a secreted precedent → protein_class 2.0 and strong cross-correction via bloodstream. Inheritance is X-linked recessive LOF (compatible), mechanism is straightforward LOF (no gate fail), and Hemgenix is approved → approval_weight 1.0. Nearly every program-comparative dimension is at or near its max, so match_score approaches the top of the scale.

**12. Why OAV101-IT and Zolgensma tie for SMA.**
They are the same product on the same vector (AAV9), same gene (SMN1), same approval status (approved), differing only in route of administration (intrathecal vs IV) and the exact tissue-target string ("CNS/spinal cord" vs "CNS/motor neuron"). The match score is built from program-comparative dimensions that don't distinguish route — packaging, tropism (both resolve to CNS), protein class, inheritance, approval, immunogenicity are identical — so they tie. The tie reveals that match_score is insensitive to delivery *route* and procedural difficulty; those considerations live in the tractability dimensions (roa_feasibility) and the review flags, not the ranking.

**13. What drags Crigler-Najjar's match score to medium.**
UGT1A1 packages easily and liver is a favourable tissue, so packaging and tropism aren't the problem. The likely drags are protein_class and pathway: UGT1A1 is an intracellular hepatocyte enzyme (not secreted), so it can't claim the full secreted/lysosomal cross-correction-style protein-class match against the secreted-protein liver precedents (Hemgenix/Roctavian), and its bilirubin-conjugation pathway is not in the same pathway group as coagulation, so pathway_similarity scores low. Top precedents are also AAV5 (moderate immunogenicity), so immunogenicity isn't maxed either — the sum lands in the medium band.

**14. Glybera's "withdrawn" = 0.7.**
Glybera was fully EMA-approved in 2012 and withdrawn in 2017 for *commercial* reasons (price/lack of demand), not safety or efficacy. As a regulatory precedent — "regulators have accepted this modality for this indication class" — a product that cleared approval is stronger evidence than an in-progress Phase 2 (0.6), which is why withdrawn is deliberately scored above phase2. The exception: a product withdrawn for *safety or efficacy* reasons should not get this score and would warrant a much lower value encoded separately.

**15. score_immunogenicity logic.**
It looks up the vector's approximate Western-adult NAb seroprevalence and maps it inversely to a 0–2 score: <10% → 2.0, <20% → 1.5, <40% → 1.0, else 0.5; lower seroprevalence = fewer excluded patients = higher score. LV (~2%) scores best because lentiviral vectors are immunologically distinct from AAVs; AAV2 (~55%) scores worst as the oldest, most naturally encountered serotype. Documented limitations include: values are Western-adult averages that ignore geographic/ancestry variation; they don't reflect engineered/synthetic capsids; they ignore lower paediatric/neonatal seroprevalence (relevant to SMA/MPS); and they don't account for immunodepletion protocols (plasmapheresis, IVIg, rapamycin) that expand eligibility.

**16. Organelle-targeting tiers.**
0.0 — mtDNA-encoded genes (MT-*): the gene lives in the mitochondrial genome, uses a non-standard genetic code, and is translated by mitochondrial ribosomes, so nuclear AAV delivery simply cannot produce the protein. 0.5 — nuclear-encoded mitochondrial matrix proteins (MUT): the gene is nuclear (AAV can deliver it) but the protein must be imported into the matrix via an intact N-terminal mitochondrial targeting sequence (MTS), which no other dimension validates. 0.7 — peroxisomal proteins: AAV-deliverable but the PTS1/PTS2 targeting signal must be preserved. "Allotopic expression" is the workaround for mtDNA genes: recode the gene for cytoplasmic translation, add an artificial MTS, and re-engineer import/assembly — a disease-specific strategy (e.g. GS010/Lumevoq for LHON) not represented in the catalog.

**17. Mean vs most-favourable tissue, and the CLI flag.**
Taking the most favourable tissue let multi-system diseases cherry-pick their easiest tissue — e.g. a kidney+CNS disease would inherit CNS's high immune-privilege/route scores while the kidney (possibly the real therapeutic target) was ignored, over-scoring tractability. Using the mean across all annotated tissues reflects average delivery difficulty. To score a single declared target tissue instead, pass `--primary-tissue` (the mean of one tissue equals that tissue's score).

**18. Pathway edge-list rebuild.**
The old hand-written dict was (a) asymmetric — A could be related to B without B being related to A — and (b) contained unsupported cross-links, notably retinal_phototransduction ↔ mitochondrial_complex, which existed only to let LHON match retinal programmes. Building the adjacency map from an undirected `_PATHWAY_EDGES` set guarantees symmetry by construction (each edge adds both A→B and B→A), so asymmetry can't recur; LHON is now handled correctly by the organelle-targeting gate instead.

**19. The therapeutic-window heuristic failure.**
The clearest example is phenylketonuria (PKU) — the textbook *wide*-window disease — which the old HPO-keyword heuristic scored in the narrowest tier because the term "intellectual disability" tripped the irreversibility branch. The heuristic matched symptom keywords rather than actual onset/progression/reversibility data, so a disease that is highly treatable with timely intervention was penalised. v4 consults curated natural-history records first and only falls back to the keyword heuristic (with a review flag) when no curated record exists.

**20. Two defensible criticisms (model viva answers).**
Scope: the catalog covers only AAV and integrating ex vivo HSC gene-*addition* programmes and explicitly excludes dual-AAV, LNP/mRNA, CRISPR/base/prime editing, ASOs and RNAi — so a disease needing one of those modalities will score low or hard-fail even when a genuinely tractable approach exists. Response: the tool flags this scope limitation explicitly, suppresses ranking for incompatible mechanisms rather than forcing a misleading match, and the score should be read as "precedent similarity within the AAV/HSC gene-addition space only." Methodology: the dimension weights and thresholds are expert-assigned heuristics, not empirically fitted, so the ranking could be sensitive to weighting choices. Response: the weights carry primary-literature citations, are exposed (MATCH_WEIGHTS/TRACTABILITY_WEIGHTS) so scores can be recomputed under perturbed weights, and scripts/sensitivity_analysis.py exists precisely to measure ranking stability — the claim is relative precedent ranking, not an absolute clinical probability.
