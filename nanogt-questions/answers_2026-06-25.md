# nanogt Answer Sheet — 2026-06-25

*Mark-scheme answers grounded in the current v4 source. Orientation note: the live scorer is v4 — 14 dimensions split into two scores (`match_score` for ranking + confidence, `tractability_score` reported separately); the catalog holds 8 vectors and 21 programmes. Some figures in `output/SUMMARY.md` and the older brief are pre-v4 (single composite /21, 46-disease cohort) and should be treated as illustrative, not current.*

---

**1. What `nanogt` takes in, produces, and its central premise.**
Input is an Orphanet disease ID (e.g. ORPHA:70). It resolves the disease (Orphanet API with curated fallback) and its causal gene (UniProt API with curated fallback), then scores every GT programme in the catalog as a candidate precedent, emitting a ranked terminal table plus a markdown report (and a combined SUMMARY.md for batch runs). The central premise: an existing approved or clinical-stage GT programme that is biologically and regulatorily similar is a usable development precedent/surrogate for a disease too rare to support its own large programme.

**2. The regulatory argument, and what a high score does NOT claim.**
Ultra-rare diseases cannot support large randomised trials and have weak commercial pull, so de novo programmes are scarce; if regulators have already accepted a modality (e.g. liver-directed AAV gene addition) for a similar indication class, that precedent de-risks the design and approval path. A high `match_score` means only "this catalog programme is the closest development template" — it does NOT mean the precedent is directly reusable, clinically validated, or that a trial is recommended. Disease-specific validation, vector engineering, toxicology and regulatory review are all still required.

**3. `match_score` vs `tractability_score`, and what was wrong with the composite.**
`match_score` (0–10) answers "how good a PRECEDENT is this specific programme for this disease?" and is built only from program-comparative dimensions that differ between candidates; it alone drives ranking and the confidence tier. `tractability_score` (0–10) answers "how amenable is this DISEASE to gene therapy at all?" from disease-intrinsic dimensions (therapeutic window, cross-correction, immune privilege, promoter availability, route feasibility, organelle targeting), which are identical for every programme — so it is computed once per disease. The old composite conflated the two: the disease-intrinsic dimensions added the same baseline to every programme, inflating the headline number and the confidence tier without ever changing which precedent ranked first.

**4. Modality coverage, exclusions, and "catalog-relative".**
The catalog covers AAV-based in vivo gene addition and ex vivo integrating HSC vectors (lentiviral, with the gammaretroviral Strimvelis grouped into the LV bucket). It explicitly excludes (any three): dual-AAV strategies for oversized genes, LNP/mRNA gene therapy, genome editing (CRISPR/base/prime), antisense oligonucleotides, RNAi, and cell+gene/transplant-enabling combinations. Every score is catalog-relative because a disease can score low here yet still be tractable through a modality not represented — absence of a strong match is not proof that no therapy is possible.

**5. The three 1.5×-weighted dimensions and why.**
`packaging_fit`, `tropism_match`, and `modality_compatibility`. They are the go/no-go decision gates: each alone can fail a programme regardless of everything else — the gene must fit the capsid, the vector must physically reach the target cell, and the mechanism must actually be amenable to gene addition. Evidence cited in the code: packaging (Wu 2010; Grieger & Samulski 2005), tropism (Nathwani 2011, where hepatic AAV8 in haemophilia B made tropism the primary selector), and modality (Keeler & Flotte 2019; Mingozzi & High 2011 — adding copies of a gain-of-function or dominant-negative gene worsens the phenotype).

**6. How `match_score` is computed, and why 17.0.**
Each of the eight program-comparative dimensions is multiplied by its weight, summed, divided by the weighted raw maximum (17.0), multiplied by 10, and rounded to two decimals (`normalise_match`). 17.0 = the three 1.5×-weighted gates at max 2.0 each (3.0 + 3.0 + 3.0 = 9.0) + protein_class 2.0 + pathway_similarity 2.0 + immunogenicity 2.0 + inheritance 1.0 + approval_weight 1.0. Weights are exposed (`MATCH_WEIGHTS`) so the sensitivity-analysis script can perturb them and measure ranking stability.

**7. Confidence tiers vs tractability tiers.**
Confidence is set on `match_score`: ≥6.0 high, ≥4.0 medium, <4.0 low; a mechanism or packaging hard-fail returns "fail" (programme excluded). Tractability is set on `tractability_score`: ≥6.5 amenable, ≥4.0 conditional, <4.0 challenging. The distinction matters because only `match_score` reflects precedent quality and is allowed to drive ranking and confidence — the tractability tier is advisory and must never reorder precedents, which is the entire point of the v4 split.

**8. The two hard gates, their order, and why mechanism first.**
Gate 1 is mechanism/modality (`gene_addition_compatibility == "incompatible"`); Gate 2 is packaging (the CDS alone exceeds the vector cargo). In v4 the mechanism gate is checked first. An incompatible mechanism (gain-of-function, dominant-negative, toxic gain-of-RNA, overexpression) means gene addition is simply the wrong modality and cannot be rescued by changing vector or shrinking the construct — the more fundamental exclusion. Worked example: Huntington (HTT) is correctly excluded by mechanism (gain-of-function) instead of being masked by an incidental packaging failure.

**9. Why SMA ranks Zolgensma / OAV101-IT at the top.**
SMN1 has a tiny 891 bp CDS, so even adding the ~1.5 kb cassette overhead it fills well under 60% of AAV9's 4,700 bp cargo → packaging 2.0. AAV9 is CNS/motor-neuron tropic and crosses the blood-brain barrier, matching SMA's CNS/muscle tissues and the programmes' motor-neuron target → strong tropism. SMA is autosomal-recessive loss-of-function in the motor_neuron pathway (inheritance match + curated LOF → modality compatible), and both programmes are approved (max approval weight), so biological fit and precedent strength are both high. Neonatal onset gives a narrow therapeutic window, but that lives in tractability and does not lower the match ranking.

**10. Why Crigler-Najjar (UGT1A1) lands below the haemophilias.**
UGT1A1 is a UDP-glucuronosyltransferase anchored in the ER membrane (microsome) — neither secreted nor a soluble lysosomal enzyme. Against the secreted/lysosomal precedents that dominate the catalog it therefore scores a protein-class mismatch and earns no cross-correction credit at the disease level, even though it is liver-directed and packages comfortably. Fewer close protein-class matches is the documented reason it sits below haemophilia A/B (whose F8/F9 are secreted clotting factors with strong systemic cross-correction).

**11. Why LHON (MT-ND4) scores organelle-targeting 0.0.**
MT-ND4 is a mitochondrial-DNA-encoded gene (symbol begins "MT-"; the disease also has mitochondrial inheritance), so standard nuclear AAV cannot produce the protein — mtDNA genes are translated by mitochondrial ribosomes using a non-standard genetic code → 0.0. The approach actually required is allotopic expression (cytoplasmic-recoded gene plus an artificial mitochondrial targeting sequence), illustrated by GS010/Lumevoq (Phase 3 completed; EMA MAA withdrawn April 2023). Because no catalog programme uses that paradigm, every precedent score for LHON is a cross-paradigm comparison only, and a review flag states this explicitly.

**12. What triggers `_packaging_gene_for_program` (DMD example).**
It triggers only when a programme's gene symbol is the same disease gene but an engineered, shortened construct — the symbol contains the disease gene name plus a marker ("micro", "mini", "truncated"), e.g. DMD → "DMD_micro". Then the relevant question becomes "is there precedent for a shortened-construct strategy?", so packaging is graded against the engineered construct (micro-dystrophin, 3,825 bp) rather than the oversized native DMD CDS (~11,055 bp, which would hard-fail), with a note that this is a micro/mini strategy and not full-length replacement.

**13. `_aggregate_tissue_mean`: before vs after v4, and the override.**
Before v4, immune privilege / promoter availability / route feasibility returned the single most favourable tissue's score, which over-scored multi-system diseases by cherry-picking the easiest tissue (e.g. a kidney+CNS disease inheriting CNS's high immune privilege while the kidney — possibly the true target — was ignored). v4 takes the mean across all annotated tissues, reflecting average delivery difficulty. The `--primary-tissue` flag overrides this by scoring a single declared target (the mean of one tissue is just that tissue).

**14. Uncurated disease/gene pair behaviour, and why "uncertain".**
`lookup_mechanism` returns an "unknown" record with `evidence_status = "missing"` and `gene_addition_compatibility = "uncertain"`. That makes the modality dimension score 1.0/2.0 (not 2.0), and `build_review_flags` raises "No curated disease-mechanism evidence; do not infer gene-addition suitability from inheritance alone." "Uncertain" is the deliberate default because inheritance can suggest loss-of-function but is not proof — it is safer to under-score an uncurated disease than to assume LOF compatibility without source-linked evidence.

**15. Why AAV9 for SMA, and why tropism is a 1.5× primary criterion.**
AAV9 has the broadest tropism of the catalog vectors (CNS, muscle, liver, heart) and crosses the blood-brain barrier, so it can reach SMA's motor neurons after systemic dosing — the basis for Zolgensma's design. Tropism is weighted 1.5× (primary) because if the vector cannot physically reach the target cell in vivo, no gene is delivered regardless of payload, packaging, or mechanism; Nathwani's hepatic AAV8 haemophilia B work established tropism as the primary selection criterion above all other dimensions.

**16. NAbs: consequence, ranking, and limitations.**
Patients with NAb titres above a threshold are excluded from AAV trials, so high seroprevalence shrinks the eligible population and raises trial complexity (and risks failed transduction). The model scores lentiviral vectors best (~2%, immunologically distinct and delivered ex vivo) and AAV5 favourably (~9%), while AAV2 scores worst (~55%, the oldest and most naturally encountered serotype). Limitations (any two): the values are Western-adult averages that ignore geographic/ancestry variation; ignore engineered/synthetic capsids; ignore the lower seroprevalence of neonates/infants (relevant to SMA and MPS); and ignore immunodepletion protocols (plasmapheresis, IVIg, rapamycin) that can expand eligibility.

**17. M6P cross-correction, and why membrane proteins score 0.0.**
A transduced cell secretes the lysosomal enzyme tagged with mannose-6-phosphate; neighbouring untransduced cells take it up through M6P receptors and route it to their own lysosomes — so a minority of corrected cells can rescue the surrounding tissue, lowering the required transduction rate (the basis of HSC approaches such as Libmeldy). This needs a soluble, secretable protein. A lysosomal *membrane* protein (MCOLN1 channel, CTNS/cystinosin transporter, NPC1) is anchored in the membrane and cannot be secreted or taken up, so cross-correction is physically impossible and the dimension scores 0.0 — every target cell must be individually transduced.

**18. The `composite_score` mismatch: what breaks and the fix.**
The v4 `ScoreBreakdown` defines `match_score` and `tractability_score` but no `composite_score`, so any code path reading `s.composite_score` raises an `AttributeError` at runtime — `generate_report`, the CLI results table, and the batch SUMMARY would all crash against the v4 scorer. (Verified: `composite_score` appears 0× in scoring.py but 3× in report.py and 4× in cli.py.) The fix is to update `report.py` and `cli.py` to use `s.match_score` for ranking/precedent display and to surface `tractability_score` separately, replacing every removed `composite_score` reference. This is the clearest "to-fix" item — repair it before demoing the CLI live.

**19. Why aa × 3 is an optimistic packaging lower bound, and where it bites.**
aa × 3 counts only the coding sequence; it omits the stop codon, 5'/3' UTRs, Kozak, promoter, polyA, and the two ITRs — roughly the ~1.5 kb cassette overhead the packaging scorer adds back — and it assumes the canonical UniProt isoform rather than the shortest therapeutically active one. So a CDS that "fits" by this estimate may not fit once the full expression cassette is built. It matters most for genes near the ~4.7 kb capsid limit — F8 (4,374 bp), CFTR (4,443), ATP7B (4,398) — and for oversized genes such as DMD (~11 kb) and MYO7A (6,648) that need micro-constructs, dual-AAV, or LV.

**20. LV/HSC dominance: real signal or artefact?**
Both. Real: soluble lysosomal enzymes cross-correct via M6P and the CNS has high immune privilege, which genuinely favours HSC-delivered secreted-enzyme strategies. Artefact: the catalog is small and over-represents LV/HSC lysosomal programmes, so they recur as the "nearest neighbour" by availability rather than proven superiority, and protein-class/pathway similarity to Libmeldy/Skysona can dominate the comparison. The tool guards against over-reading this with explicit catalog-bias notes ("ranking is catalog-relative… extrapolating from indirect precedents"), targeted review flags (e.g. lysosomal-membrane proteins are platform-only comparisons), and by keeping disease amenability (tractability) separate from precedent quality (match).
