NanoGT: A Computational Framework for Gene Therapy Precedent Matching in Rare Diseases

Lili Yassin  ·  Department of Bioengineering / Faculty of Medicine, Imperial College London
MSc Dissertation · Personalised Medicine & Novel Therapies · Supervisor: [Supervisor Name] · 2025–2026
Dissertation Days 2026 Imperial College London
Abstract: Approximately 7,000 rare diseases exist yet the vast majority lack approved gene therapies, despite the platform potential of AAV and lentiviral vectors. The bottleneck is not biology — it is the lack of a systematic method to identify which existing approved gene-therapy program provides the best biological and regulatory precedent for an untreated disease. NanoGT is a Python CLI framework that, given an Orphanet disease identifier, queries public databases, scores 21 approved or clinical-stage gene therapy programs across 14 biologically and clinically relevant dimensions, and returns a ranked precedent list with confidence classification and plain-language rationale. Validated across a 46-disease proof-of-concept cohort — including positive controls, stress tests, and non-loss-of-function mechanisms — NanoGT correctly recovers known clinical precedents and identifies a systematic gap: many loss-of-function diseases with feasible AAV delivery have no active therapy programme, suggesting economics, not biology, is the primary barrier.
Background & Problem

The Rare Disease Treatment Gap

~7,000 distinct rare diseases are identified globally. Collectively they affect hundreds of millions of people, yet only a small fraction have disease-modifying treatments. For nano-rare diseases (prevalence < 1 in 1,000,000), commercial return is vanishingly small and diseases are systematically deprioritised by industry — leaving patients with no approved therapy, no active trial, and no funded research programme.

Gene Therapy as a Platform Solution

Landmark approvals — Luxturna (2017), Zolgensma (2019), Hemgenix (2022) — established that single-administration gene therapy can produce durable correction across diverse tissue targets. Critically, the foundational regulatory and safety work for one vector-tissue combination becomes a shared resource: a subsequent programme using the same vector in the same tissue can leverage this established precedent, dramatically reducing development costs.

The Precedent Problem For a given untreated disease, which existing approved programme is the best biological and regulatory precedent? This assessment is currently performed manually, is time-consuming, and is inaccessible to small patient groups. No public computational tool integrates the full range of relevant parameters to make this assessment systematically.
Methods — NanoGT Framework

System Architecture

NanoGT is a Python CLI tool (nanogt match [ORPHA-ID]) that: (1) queries Orphanet for disease phenotype and causal gene, (2) retrieves gene properties from UniProt, (3) scores 21 programs in a curated surrogate database across 14 dimensions, and (4) outputs a ranked match list with composite scores and a Markdown/PDF report.

Stress Tests & Edge Cases

  • DMD (oversized cargo): CDS = 11,055 bp; framework correctly penalises standard AAV and returns micro-dystrophin precedent (SRP-9001) at 7.6/10 — confirming the packaging gate functions.
  • LHON (mitochondrial): MT-ND4 is mitochondrially encoded; allotopic delivery required. Score appropriately reduced to 6.4/10 (medium).
  • NF1 (haploinsufficiency, 8.4 kb): Hard packaging fail — no single-vector precedent survives. Correctly flags dual-vector or non-viral approach needed.
Key Findings

If a disease has clear loss-of-function biology, an AAV-deliverable gene, and a tractable target tissue — but no gene therapy programme exists — the gap is likely economics and prevalence, not biology. NanoGT makes this gap visible and quantifiable.

Biological Findings

  • Lysosomal cluster: Diseases sharing secreted lysosomal enzyme biology (MPS I, II, IIIA, MLD, Krabbe, Gaucher, Fabry) converge on Libmeldy/LV as top precedent — cross-correction biology is a powerful score driver.
  • Liver metabolic cluster: Hepatic enzyme disorders (Crigler-Najjar, OTC deficiency, PKU, methylmalonic acidaemia) cluster around BMN 307/AAV5 — established liver tropism and manufacturing precedent transfers broadly.
  • Retinal cluster: Luxturna/AAV2 and CPCB-RPE1/AAV8 capture most retinal LOF diseases — immune-privileged compartment and cone/rod tropism are decisive.
  • Membrane proteins score lower: MCOLN1 (Mucolipidosis IV) and NPC1 cannot exploit cross-correction; per-cell delivery requirement increases complexity and reduces confidence.

Non-LoF Extension

The framework was extended to five non-loss-of-function mechanisms: haploinsufficiency (Rett, TSC, NF1, CHARGE), and repeat-expansion silencing (Fragile X, Friedreich ataxia). Friedreich ataxia scores 7.9/10 — cDNA addition bypasses the silenced endogenous allele, making gene addition mechanistically appropriate. NF1 fails the packaging gate, correctly flagging a fundamental constraint.

Novel finding — Organelle targeting dimension Standard frameworks miss a category error: proteins requiring delivery to specific subcellular compartments (mitochondria, peroxisomes) must have intact targeting signals in the therapeutic construct. NanoGT scores this explicitly. Primary hyperoxaluria type 1 (AGXT, a peroxisomal enzyme) and methylmalonic acidaemia (MMUT, a mitochondrial matrix enzyme) receive appropriate caveats.
Limitations
  • Surrogate database is curated (21 programs) — not comprehensive. Clinical-stage programmes may be missed.
  • Scoring is heuristic, not probabilistic. Weights are biologically motivated but not trained on outcome data.
  • Disease biology relies on Orphanet curation and OMIM annotations — errors in source data propagate.
  • Framework does not assess manufacturability, immunogenicity at individual patient level, or trial feasibility.
  • Not a clinical decision tool — outputs require expert review and disease-specific validation before any therapeutic inference.
Conclusions
  • NanoGT correctly identifies clinically validated precedents for all positive control diseases, confirming the 14-dimension framework is biologically coherent.
  • The 46-disease cohort reveals systematic clustering of rare diseases around a small number of approved vector-tissue combinations — the foundational precedent work already done could benefit many untreated diseases.
  • Diseases where LoF is clear, AAV delivery is feasible, and no programme exists represent gaps attributable to economics rather than biology — a result meaningful in itself.
  • NanoGT provides an open, extensible, public-database-driven tool that makes precedent identification accessible to patient groups and small academic teams.
