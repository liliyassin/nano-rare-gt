# nanogt Answer Sheet — 2026-06-28

**1. The v4 algorithm replaced the single composite score with two separate scores. Name both, say what each one measures, and state which one drives the ranking and the confidence tier — and why the other one must NOT be blended into the ranking.** *(conceptual)*

The two scores are **match_score** (0–10) and **tractability_score** (0–10). match_score measures how good a *precedent* a specific program is for the disease, built only from program-comparative dimensions (packaging, tropism, modality, protein class, pathway, inheritance, approval, immunogenicity); it drives both the ranking and the confidence tier. tractability_score measures how amenable the *disease itself* is to gene therapy, from disease-intrinsic dimensions (therapeutic window, cross-correction, immune privilege, promoter availability, route feasibility, organelle targeting). Because the tractability dimensions are identical for every candidate program, blending them into the ranking would add the same constant to every program (leaving rank order unchanged) while inflating the headline number and pushing scores across the confidence thresholds — the exact v3 flaw that v4 fixes.

**2. What regulatory/translational question is nanogt actually trying to answer, and why is the "precedent" framing useful?** *(conceptual)*

It asks: for a rare loss-of-function disease with little or no dedicated program, which existing clinically/regulatory-validated GT program is the closest development template — i.e. what vector, route, and modality would a developer most defensibly start from? Precedent framing is useful because developers and regulators reason by analogy to approved products, so a strong precedent de-risks the vector choice, tropism assumptions, immunogenicity profile, and the regulatory path.

**3. What mechanisms keep the "not directly reusable" interpretation honest?** *(conceptual)*

Three things. (a) **review_flags** — translational caveats kept deliberately separate from confidence (lysosomal membrane protein, mtDNA gene, MTS-validation-needed, multi-subunit enzyme, unresolved biology, multi-system tissue, etc.), so a program can be the best precedent and still need manual review. (b) The **tractability tier**, which is advisory and never affects ranking. (c) The **catalog scope-limitation note**. The interpretation line itself states the top precedent is not reusable without disease-specific validation, vector engineering, toxicology, and regulatory review.

**4. Why restrict the catalog to AAV / ex vivo LV-HSC gene addition, and what does a low score NOT prove?** *(conceptual)*

Because the scoring logic only models gene-addition biology (packaging, tropism, cross-correction, organelle targeting); mixing in editing, ASO, RNAi, LNP-mRNA or dual-AAV programs would compare mechanistically incommensurable approaches. A low score therefore means only "weak precedent *within the AAV/HSC gene-addition space*" — it does not prove the disease is GT-intractable, because a better route may exist via a modality not in the catalog (e.g. editing for a dominant-negative disease, dual-AAV for an oversized gene).

**5. Which three dimensions carry the 1.5× weight, what category do they share, and why?** *(algorithm/scoring)*

packaging_fit, tropism_match, and modality_compatibility. They share the category of **go/no-go decision gates** — the dimensions most predictive of program failure in the literature. A gene that doesn't fit the capsid can't be delivered by that platform; a vector that doesn't reach the target tissue delivers nothing; and an incompatible mechanism means gene addition is the wrong modality altogether. (Cited: Srivastava 2016 / Grieger & Samulski 2005 for packaging; Nathwani 2011 for tropism; Keeler & Flotte 2019 / Mingozzi & High 2011 for modality.)

**6. Give the confidence and tractability tier thresholds, and which score each is set on.** *(algorithm/scoring)*

Confidence (set on **match_score**): ≥ 6.0 → high, ≥ 4.0 → medium, < 4.0 → low, plus **fail** for any program that trips a hard gate. Tractability (set on **tractability_score**): ≥ 6.5 → amenable, ≥ 4.0 → conditional, < 4.0 → challenging. Confidence reports precedent strength and drives the ranking display; the tractability tier is advisory only.

**7. Which hard gate is now checked first, why, and an example it fixes.** *(algorithm/scoring)*

The **mechanism / modality gate** is now checked before the packaging gate. It's the more fundamental exclusion: an incompatible mechanism (gain-of-function, dominant-negative, toxic gain-of-RNA, overexpression) means gene addition is simply the wrong approach and cannot be rescued by changing the vector or shrinking the construct, whereas a packaging failure is potentially solvable. Example: Huntington (HTT) — it should be excluded on mechanism, not masked by an incidental packaging pass/fail, which is what the reorder guarantees.

**8. Why do the hard-fail and the fill grade use different lengths, and what are the fill bands?** *(algorithm/scoring)*

The absolute hard-fail uses the **bare CDS** because the coding sequence cannot be shortened without re-engineering — if it alone exceeds the capsid, no promoter trick rescues it, so the program is rejected. The fill grade uses **CDS + ~1.5 kb overhead** (≈ 2× ITR ~290 bp + promoter + UTRs/Kozak + polyA), because the full expression cassette is what actually has to be packaged. Bands: fill ≤ 0.60 → 2.0 (comfortable); ≤ 0.85 → 1.5 (good); ≤ 1.00 → 1.0 (tight, near capsid limit); > 1.00 → 0.5 (CDS fits but cassette doesn't — needs minimal-promoter/engineered cassette, dual-AAV, or a higher-capacity vector, e.g. B-domain-deleted F8 or micro-dystrophin).

**9. SMA / SMN1 — why the top precedents rank high, the cross-correction limit, and which score each fact feeds.** *(disease-specific)*

SMN1 is tiny (891 bp) so packaging_fit is maximal, and AAV9 (Zolgensma, OAV101-IT) is CNS/motor-neuron tropic, matching SMA's target tissue — both are program-comparative dimensions, so they feed **match_score** and put those AAV9 programs at the top. But SMN1 is an intracellular/nuclear protein: it can't be secreted, so cross_correction scores low (0.2) and every target motor neuron must be individually transduced. Cross-correction is disease-intrinsic, so that limitation feeds the **tractability_score**, not the ranking — SMA looks like a strong precedent match while still carrying a cell-autonomous-delivery caveat.

**10. Fabry (GLA) vs ML-IV (MCOLN1) — the v2 protein-class distinction and its mechanistic basis.** *(disease-specific)*

GLA is a soluble lysosomal **enzyme** (also secreted), so against an enzyme precedent it scores protein_class 2.0 and high cross_correction — M6P-pathway cross-correction is viable. MCOLN1 is a lysosomal **membrane** channel (TRP family); the v2 fix separates membrane proteins from soluble enzymes, so it scores protein_class 1.0 and cross_correction 0.0. Mechanistically, HSC/enzyme precedents (Libmeldy/ARSA) work because microglia secrete the enzyme and neurons re-import it via M6P receptors — physically impossible for a membrane-anchored channel, which must be delivered to every individual cell.

**11. LHON / MT-ND4 — why AAV can't treat it, the real precedent, and how the framework surfaces it.** *(disease-specific)*

MT-ND4 is encoded in mitochondrial DNA and translated by mitochondrial ribosomes using a non-standard genetic code, so a nucleus-delivered AAV transgene cannot produce a functional protein at the target site — hence organelle_targeting = 0.0. The relevant real-world strategy is **allotopic expression** (the gene recoded for cytoplasmic translation plus an artificial mitochondrial targeting sequence), exemplified by **GS010/Lumevoq** (Phase 3 completed; EMA MAA voluntarily withdrawn April 2023 after the primary endpoint was not met vs sham). The 0.0 dimension plus a "mitochondrial DNA gene" review flag makes the incompatibility numerically explicit, so LHON no longer earns a high score by false analogy to nuclear gene-addition programs.

**12. What score does an uncurated mechanism yield, and why is under-scoring deliberate?** *(code/implementation)*

An uncurated pair returns the "uncertain / missing" record, which makes score_gene_addition_compatibility return **1.0/2.0** (vs 2.0 for a curated "compatible"). Under-scoring is the deliberate choice because inheritance can *suggest* loss-of-function but is not proof of it — the framework refuses to assume gene-addition suitability without a source-linked mechanism row. Better to under-score an uncurated disease than to over-claim compatibility it cannot defend; a "no curated mechanism evidence" review flag is also raised.

**13. Why prefer the curated local fallback over the live API for cohort genes?** *(code/implementation)*

For cohort entries the curated fallback is tried first so results are **reproducible and stable** — not subject to UniProt search-ranking changes, isoform selection, or network availability — which matters for a dissertation where the same query must produce the same numbers on every run. The live Orphanet/UniProt path still exists so the same code can score arbitrary monogenic diseases outside the cohort; the curated-first rule simply protects the audited cohort from silently shifting underneath the analysis.

**14. The v4-engine vs CLI/report inconsistency, and what `nanogt match ORPHA:324` would do today.** *(code/implementation)*

The v4 ScoreBreakdown exposes **match_score** and **tractability_score** and has no **composite_score** attribute, yet cli.py (lines ~152/236/336/352) and report.py (lines ~237/251/271) still format `s.composite_score` (and models.py / schema.sql still define the field). So running `nanogt match ORPHA:324` would raise an **AttributeError** when it tries to print `s.composite_score` — the v4 refactor of the scoring engine was never propagated to the CLI/report/DB layer. *(Verify/fix before any live viva demo: update those call sites to match_score and surface tractability_score separately.)*

**15. Rank AAV2, AAV5, AAV9, LV by immunogenicity score, and why low seroprevalence scores high.** *(external knowledge)*

Best → worst: **LV** (~2%, score 2.0) > **AAV5** (~9%, score 2.0) > **AAV9** (~22%, score 1.0) > **AAV2** (~55%, score 0.5). Low seroprevalence earns a high score because patients with pre-existing neutralising antibodies are excluded from AAV trials — a vector that few people carry antibodies against means more eligible patients and simpler trial design. (Both LV and AAV5 fall in the < 0.10 band, so both score 2.0; the band sets the score, not the exact value.)

**16. What does aa_length × 3 under-count, and why does it matter near the packaging limit?** *(external knowledge)*

It omits the stop codon (+3 bp), the 5′/3′ UTRs (~200–500 bp), and assumes the canonical UniProt isoform rather than the shortest therapeutically active one — and of course it excludes the promoter, ITRs, and polyA, which are added at packaging. This matters near the ~4.7 kb AAV ceiling because a CDS that *looks* like it fits can exceed cargo once UTRs and regulatory elements are added — exactly the situation the ~1.5 kb cassette-overhead term in score_packaging is designed to catch. A borderline gene needs sequence-confirmed construct data, not the aa×3 estimate.

**17. Define cross-correction, name the pathway, and explain why a membrane transporter can't use it.** *(external knowledge)*

Cross-correction is when a transduced cell secretes the therapeutic protein and neighbouring untransduced cells take it up, so a minority of corrected cells can rescue the majority (lowering the required transduction rate and dose). For soluble lysosomal enzymes it works via the **mannose-6-phosphate (M6P) receptor pathway**: the enzyme is secreted M6P-tagged and recaptured by M6P receptors on neighbouring lysosomes. A lysosomal membrane transporter/channel (CTNS, MCOLN1, NPC1) is anchored in the membrane and cannot be exocytosed as soluble cargo, so it cannot cross-correct — every target cell must be individually transduced (scored 0.0).

**18. Why can't an identical-across-programs dimension change rank order, yet still distort confidence?** *(critical thinking)*

Ranking compares candidate programs against each other for a single disease; a disease-intrinsic dimension takes the same value for every program, so including it shifts every candidate's total by the same constant and leaves their relative order unchanged. It still distorts the **headline number** and can push scores across the *absolute* confidence thresholds — e.g. lifting a mediocre precedent from "medium" to "high" — so the disease's general GT-amenability ends up masquerading as precedent quality. v4 removes the distortion by reporting tractability separately and basing confidence solely on match_score.

**19. The "best tissue → mean" change: the failure mode, the override flag, and a trade-off.** *(critical thinking)*

"Best tissue" let a multi-system disease inherit the score of its *easiest* tissue — e.g. a kidney+CNS disease taking CNS's high immune-privilege/promoter scores while the kidney (possibly the real therapeutic target) was ignored — over-scoring tractability by cherry-picking. v4 uses the **mean across all annotated tissues**; the **--primary-tissue** flag overrides it by declaring a single target (the mean of one tissue equals that tissue). Trade-off: the mean can *under-score* a disease where only one tissue actually needs treating, which is precisely why the override exists.

**20. The therapeutic-window heuristic failure, why it failed, and why a wrong window can't reorder ranking.** *(critical thinking)*

The textbook example is **phenylketonuria (PKU)**: the keyword heuristic scored the textbook *wide*-window disease in the *narrowest* tier because the term "intellectual disability" tripped the irreversibility branch. v4 consults curated natural-history data first (mapping window_class → score) and only falls back to the keyword heuristic, with a review flag, when no curated record exists. Even a wrong window value cannot reorder precedents because therapeutic_window feeds only the disease-level **tractability_score**, which is identical across every program and never enters the match ranking.
